# Section 6: Discussion — Data Analysis & Interpretation

## Core Idea
The authors argue their tomographic findings are consistent with (and extend) prior thermal, microgravimetric, and muon-based hints of hidden pyramid structures, then propose a speculative hydraulic/acoustic "function" for the whole internal system — explicitly framed by the authors themselves as a starting hypothesis, not a proven conclusion.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Cross-method consistency argument**: rather than claiming novelty in isolation, the authors map each new structure onto specific prior independent findings (thermal anomaly studies, microgravimetry, 1998 electrogravitic measurements, 2017 muon spectroscopy) to argue convergent evidence.
  - When to use: when presenting a new measurement method's results, explicitly cross-reference every independent prior partial finding it does or doesn't corroborate — including explicit non-matches (see Anti-patterns).
- **Speculative-function narrative (Quincke's tube / Helmholtz resonator hydraulic model)**: a hypothesized water-and-acoustic system in which the pyramid was flooded via the Nile up to the level of a slope-change on its faces, water circulated through the King's/Queen's Chamber shafts (called a "Quincke's tube" closed circuit) and the Zed acted as a multi-stage low-pass acoustic filter, with two individuals allegedly treated with resulting low-frequency sound for "curative and religious purposes."
  - How presented: built from the 8-facet exterior finding (Section 5.1), the ramp/room network (Section 5, tags 1–4, 11, 14, 17, 19, 20), and analogy to acoustic filter physics (Quincke tube, Helmholtz resonator) cited from the authors' own prior work.

## Key Concepts
- **Thermal anomaly correlation**: prior researchers found a thermal anomaly at the pyramid's NE ground-level edge suggesting a hidden room/corridor; this paper's tags 1 and 4 (ramps) are argued to explain that anomaly's location.
- **Microgravimetry non-homogeneity**: prior gravity surveys under the King's Chamber floor found density anomalies; mapped here to tags 12/13.
- **Explicit non-match**: the 2017 muon-spectroscopy "big void" was **not** detected by this paper's SAR method at the same reported location — the authors note this discrepancy directly rather than omitting it, while questioning the muon study's reliability per an Egyptian Supreme Council of Antiquities assessment.
- **Processing cost**: a full-resolution tomogram took ~6 days of computation on a Dell i7 workstation with 32 GB RAM — cited by the authors as the method's main practical disadvantage.

## Anti-patterns
- **Silently dropping disagreements between methods**: the authors' explicit acknowledgment that they did *not* detect the muon-reported "big void" at its claimed location is presented as a discrepancy to be reasoned about (possible interpretation error vs. absence), not swept aside — a practice worth emulating when synthesizing multi-method evidence.
- **Presenting interpretation as settled fact**: the water-circuit/acoustic-therapy narrative in 6.2 is explicitly labeled by the authors as "only intended to be a starting point for further interpretative ideas" — treat this section as hypothesis-generation, not a validated mechanism.

## Key Takeaways
1. The paper distinguishes **direct evidence** (their own SAR-derived geometries) from **indirect hypotheses** (all prior non-SAR methods), positioning SAR tomography as qualitatively stronger evidence — a framing claim, not an independently adjudicated one.
2. One explicit non-corroboration (2017 muon "big void") is acknowledged rather than hidden.
3. The functional/hydraulic interpretation (water circuit, Quincke's tube, Helmholtz resonance, therapeutic use) is a separate, more speculative layer built atop the structural catalog — worth keeping conceptually distinct from the tomographic measurements themselves.
4. Computational cost (~6 days/tomogram) is flagged as the primary practical limitation of the method.
