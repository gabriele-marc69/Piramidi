# Section 2: Giza Plateau & Known Interior Structures

## Core Idea
Before presenting new findings, the paper establishes the baseline: the three Giza pyramids' layout, and Khufu's *already-known* interior features (King's Chamber, Queen's Chamber, Grand Gallery, unfinished chamber, the "Zed" monument, air ducts) that any new tomographic result must be consistent with.

## Key Concepts
- **The Zed**: a large granite monument sitting above the King's Chamber, built from an angled two-slab roof plus five parallel spaced granite slabs (rough top faces, smooth bottom faces) — distinct from the conventional "relieving chambers" framing.
- **King's Chamber**: off-axis (south of apex), contains the sarcophagus, sits below the Zed.
- **Queen's Chamber**: on-axis, smaller, located below the King's Chamber.
- **Unfinished Room**: off-axis, underground, south-shifted.
- **Grand Gallery**: large corridor connecting King's and Queen's Chambers.
- **Air ducts / shafts**: multiple ducts connecting the Grand Gallery to the Queen's Chamber and the unfinished room.

## Key Takeaways
1. All three Giza pyramids (Khufu, Kefren, Menkaure) are covered by the external InSAR analysis (Section 5.1); only Khufu is covered by internal tomography.
2. The "Zed" is treated by the authors as a distinct structural/acoustic element, not merely relieving chambers — this framing matters for their later acoustic-resonance interpretation (Section 6.2).
3. This baseline diagram is the control against which the paper's 20 new tagged structures (Section 5, Table 3) are laid out spatially.
